# Hero Video Script

## 1. Brief

| | |
|---|---|
| **Goal** | Show a first-time visitor that the breakdowns the pros do on TV are now something they can do themselves, with Tapp'd |
| **Audience** | Coaches, analysts, creators, fans |
| **Length** | ~79s, 90s at most (Act 0 10s, Act 2 12.5s, Act 3 51.3s, Act 4 5s) |
| **Format** | _TBD_ 16:9 MP4, or a live animated component |
| **Data** | _TBD_ Real tracking data (e.g. the Western Sydney v Wellington goal) or choreographed |
| **Sound** | None. The video is silent: captions and visual cues carry the story |
| **Look** | Flat, geometric cartoon (Option A). Act 0's TV channels washed out; Tapp'd's full colours arrive on the laptop in Act 2 |

## 2. Core message

- **One line:** "Turn Match Data Into Tactical Stories." (current landing headline)
- **Story:** On TV, the pros make breaking down a play look easy → Ever wanted to do it yourself? → Tapp'd: now it's your turn, on your own laptop: the match becomes data → Find, analyse, tell the story

## 3. Acts

Times are given within each act (each act starts at 0.0s).

| Act | Name | Time (approx.) |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | Cold open: channel-surfing the pros | ~10s |
| 2 | Enter Tapp'd: a full-screen intro, then the viewer opens a laptop and the match turns into the Tapp'd plot | 12.5s |
| 3 | The payoff: five features, each introduced by a title card on black, then done in the app with the cursor | 51.3s |
| 4 | Close: logo, headline, call to action | 5s |

## Act 0: Cold open: channel-surfing the pros (0.0–10.0s)

**Job:** Open on a line that grabs attention, then show something every viewer has seen: pundits on TV breaking down a play. Three clicks of the remote show that it happens across every sport. Then turn it on the viewer: "Ever wanted to do it yourself?" Act 2 answers it.

**Set:** A living room at night, seen from behind the couch. A flat TV on the far wall is the only light, so the room is in its blue-grey glow. In the foreground: the back of a viewer's head and shoulders above the couch, and their hand holding a remote in the lower right.

**Cast:**
- **Viewer:** back to camera, slouched, arm resting on the couch arm with the remote. Only the head, shoulders and remote hand are seen.
- **Three pundits** (on the TV only): generic cartoon figures, not real people.

**Through-line:** The remote. Every cut is a press of the thumb, shown visually since there is no sound: the button dips and the IR light blinks.

**Look on the TV:** The channels are washed out: desaturated, slightly faded, with faint scanlines. They read as "broadcast TV", and Tapp'd's colours in Act 2 stay the brightest thing in the video.

**Branding:** All made up. A generic channel bug (`SPORTS 4`) and a plain score bar with invented team names. No network, league or club logos, no real player names, no recognisable pundits.

**Callback in Act 3:** Each channel does one thing Tapp'd later lets the viewer do: drawing on the play (CH 4), pointing at a player (CH 7), moving players around (CH 9).

### Shot 0.0: Title card (0.0–1.5s)

- **Screen:** Black. **"You've seen the pros break down a play."** fades in, centred, in the same style as the closing question.
- **Hand-off:** As the line fades out, the couch scene fades up from black.

### Shot 0.1: The couch (1.4–2.2s)

- **Camera:** Behind the couch. The TV sits at about 55% of the frame width.
- **Action:** The TV shows static. The viewer's hand lifts the remote and points it.
- **Caption:** none

### The channel change (used between every shot below, ~0.2s)

1. The thumb presses down, the button dips and the remote's IR light blinks.
2. Static fills the screen for 3 frames, then the picture rolls in with a quick colour split for 2 frames.
3. The new channel settles. A green, VCR-style channel number (`CH 04`) shows in the TV's top-right for 1s.

### Shot 0.2: CH 4: American football scribbles (2.2–4.2s)

- **Camera:** Slow push-in on the TV, which continues through 0.2–0.4.
- **On the TV:** A wide broadcast angle of the line of scrimmage, frozen just before the snap. Linemen in two invented team colours, a stadium crowd as a band of dots. A plain score bar along the bottom.
- **Action:** Yellow marker strokes draw themselves over the play, fast and loose: two sweeping lines across the line, a third diving at the quarterback, then a big loopy circle scribbled round and round one player.
- **Caption:** none. The channels play without captions, so nothing covers the viewer or the TV.

### Shot 0.3: CH 7: basketball studio (4.2–6.2s)

- **On the TV:** A studio stage with a giant screen on the back wall showing a court mid-play. A pundit in a light suit, back to camera, stands in front of it.
- **Action:**
  1. The pundit walks a few steps along the stage and points up at the screen.
  2. The play on the giant screen freezes. A white ring pops round one player, and a short arrow shows where they're going.

### Shot 0.4: CH 9: soccer tactics board (6.2–8.2s)

- **On the TV:** A giant touchscreen showing a top-down pitch with player discs in two invented colours, numbers only. A pundit's arm reaches in from the lower left.
- **Action:** The pundit's finger presses one disc and drags it across the pitch, leaving a dashed trail and an arrowhead where it lands.

### Shot 0.5: Switch off (8.2–10.0s)

- **Action:**
  1. The thumb presses again and the TV switches off: the picture squashes to a bright horizontal line, then a dot, then black. The room goes dark with it.
  2. On black: **"Ever wanted to do it yourself?"** (8.5–9.9s), centred and larger than the other captions, since the screen is empty.
- **Hand-off to Act 2:** Stays on black. Act 2 answers at once with Tapp'd's full-screen intro.

## Act 2: Enter Tapp'd (0.0–12.5s)

**Job:** Answer "Ever wanted to do it yourself?" straight away with Tapp'd, then show it's yours: the viewer opens their own laptop, and the match on it turns into Tapp'd's plot.

### Shot 2.1: Tapp'd, full screen (0.0–3.0s)

- **Action:**
  1. Straight out of Act 0's black, a navy backdrop fades up.
  2. The Tapp'd logo (the real mark, with white ink) and the `TAPP'D` wordmark scale in, centred.
  3. Below them: **"Now it's your turn."**, in Barlow Condensed, with "your turn." in orange.
  4. Fade to black.

**Set (2.2 on):** The same living room at night, now seen over the viewer's right shoulder. A laptop sits on their lap, hands on the keys. Act 0's TV is off in the background, softly out of focus, partly hidden behind the laptop. The laptop screen is the only light: it lights the keys, the hands and the side of the viewer's head, first in the match's muted green, then in Tapp'd's brighter greens.

### Shot 2.2: Open the laptop (3.0–4.7s)

- **Action:** The room fades up dimly with the laptop closed. The lid swings open: first its grey back, then the dark screen as it stands up.

### Shot 2.3: The screen wakes (4.7–6.5s)

- **Action:** The screen fades up on a live match at a broadcast angle (`BLU 0 – 0 RED`, `67:09`). Its light spills over the keys, hands and the viewer. The pass is played and the runner bursts onto it.

### Shot 2.4: Broadcast → plot, Tapp'd takes over (6.5–10.4s)

- **On the screen:**
  1. The broadcast angle tilts up into a top-down view of the whole pitch. The stands and score bar fall away, the players collapse into numbered dots, and colour floods in.
  2. Tapp'd's markers switch on: a yellow ring on the player with the ball, cyan rings on passing options, a pink ring on the defender pressing, and an orange dashed line for an off-ball run.
  3. The Tapp'd top bar drops in.
- **Action:** The viewer leans in.

### Shot 2.5: Into the app (10.4–12.5s)

- **Camera:** Pushes into the laptop screen until it fills the frame. A beat on the app, then fade to black for Act 3's first title card.

## Act 3: The payoff (0.0–51.3s)

**Job:** Show what the viewer can now do themselves, by doing it in the app: find a moment in soccer terms, mark it up, read the numbers, build it into a clip and save it.

**Set:** Tapp'd full screen, laid out like the real app: the top bar, the **left rail** (Build: Moments, Insights, Overlays, Settings; Draw: Player Focus, Link Players, Team Shape, Draw Rectangle, Draw Line, Draw Arrow, Erase), the plot, and the timeline under it once it's needed. An open panel (Moments, Overlays) takes the rail's place, with a Back button, and the plot shrinks beside it. The chosen move: the pass to the runner (blue 11), the run in behind, and a goal at the end.

**Through-line:** The gap. Every beat points at the same thing: the space behind red's left-back that the runner attacks.

**The cursor:** Everything except 3.1 is done by the cursor, in as few clicks as it takes, with a beat after each click so the viewer can follow what was clicked and what it did. Buttons, cards and rows light up as it hovers over them.

**Title cards:** Each feature is introduced before it is shown. The app fades to black, a single line fades in at the centre, in the same style as Act 0's cards (Helvetica Neue bold, cream), with the last word in Tapp'd orange (`#fe7f2d`). It fades out, and the black fades away to the feature. Each card is about 1.9s. The app holds its state underneath, so each feature picks up where the last one left off.

| Act 0 channel | Act 3 beat |
|---|---|
| CH 4: scribbling on a frozen frame | 3.1: four kinds of annotation, all moving with the play |
| CH 7: pointing at one player | 3.3: Player Focus on the scorer |

### Card: "Find the **moment.**" (0.0–1.6s)

Act 3 opens on black, straight from Act 2.

### Shot 3.0: Find the moment (1.6–6.9s)

Three clicks.

- **Action:**
  1. The app, with a match playing live.
  2. The cursor clicks **Moments** on the rail. The Moments panel opens in its place (`12 moments`) and the plot shrinks beside it.
  3. It clicks the `67:05 · 0:06 · Goal` moment ("BLU build-up v RED mid block"). It's tagged `In clip`, and the plot jumps to the start of the move.
  4. A beat, then it clicks **Back** and the rail returns.

### Card: "Mark up the **play.**" (6.9–8.8s)

### Shot 3.1: Mark up the play (8.8–15.0s)

A showcase of what the Draw tools and overlays draw. No cursor.

- **Action:**
  1. The plot splits into a 2×2 grid beside the rail: four copies of the same move playing in sync.
  2. Each cell's annotation draws itself on, one after another, with an app-style label in its corner:
     - **Pitch Control:** the pitch shades by team. A pocket of blue opens behind red's left-back and grows as the runner heads into it.
     - **Team Shape:** an outline joins red's back six, with its width × depth readout.
     - **Link Players:** lines join red's back four, each labelled with its distance. The left-back–centre-back gap stretches as the left-back follows blue 7 wide.
     - **Player Focus:** a spotlight on the runner, with an invented name label (`J. ARLO`) and a fading trail.
  3. All four play on together, so it's clear the marks follow the players, until blue 8 has the ball.
  4. The grid closes back into one plot, keeping Pitch Control on.

### Card: "Read the **numbers.**" (15.0–16.9s)

### Shot 3.2: Read the numbers (16.9–28.2s)

- **Action:**
  1. Play is paused with blue 8 on the ball. Pitch Control fades off and the **Insights** timeline rises under the plot: the xThreat track, the Goals row, and a play button.
  2. The cursor clicks **Overlays** on the rail. The Overlays panel opens: Pitch Control, Pass Probability, Sequence Trails, Voronoi, Event Map, Pass Network.
  3. It clicks **Pass Probability**, which is marked `On`. Lines draw out from blue 8 to each passing option, labelled `82%`, `64%`, `31%`.
  4. It clicks **Back**. The **xThreat** readout appears in the top-right corner (`0.11`). A beat to read the numbers.
  5. The cursor clicks **play**. The pass to the runner is played along the `64%` line, and xThreat climbs with every touch (`0.11 → 0.27 → 0.58`), pulsing at each step, while the xThreat track draws itself under the playhead.
  6. The shot. The readout peaks and a **Goal** marker drops onto the Goals row.

### Card: "Build the **clip.**" (28.2–30.1s)

### Shot 3.3: Build the clip (30.1–46.5s)

Add an overlay, play on, follow the scorer, watch it through.

- **Layout:** The timeline grows to show the Clip rows (Moments, Pitch Control, Pass Probability, Player Focus), and the playhead jumps back to the start of the clip. The moment picked in 3.0 is already in the **Moments** row, and the Pass Probability turned on in 3.2 is already a bar, from where it was switched on.
- **Action:**
  1. **Overlay:** The cursor clicks **Overlays**, then **Pitch Control** (`On`). The pitch shades and a Pitch Control bar drops into its row. It clicks **Back**.
  2. **Play on:** It clicks **play**. The move plays with Pitch Control on, and the passing options flash up as blue 8 gets the ball.
  3. **Follow the scorer:** As the pass is played to the runner, it clicks **pause**, picks **Player Focus** on the rail (the button stays lit) and clicks blue 11. Spotlight and name label (`J. ARLO`), and a Player Focus bar drops in from that point.
  4. **Watch it:** It clicks **play**. The runner, in the spotlight, takes the pass, cuts inside and scores, to the end of the clip.

### Card: "Save it. **Share it.**" (46.5–48.4s)

### Shot 3.4: Save it (48.4–51.3s)

- **Action:**
  1. The cursor clicks **Save Project** in the top bar. The button's dot turns into a tick and a `Project saved` toast appears.
  2. The app shrinks back into a framed card at the centre of the screen, and the background goes navy. That hands off to the close.

## Act 4: Close (0.0–5.0s)

**Job:** Leave the viewer with what Tapp'd is and what to do next.

**Set:** Act 3's last frame: the saved project as a card on the navy backdrop.

- **0.0–1.0s:** The "Project saved" toast fades. The card settles smaller and higher, and the finished clip starts replaying inside it, with every overlay switching on and off as its bar comes round.
- **0.6–1.3s:** The Tapp'd logo (the real mark from `frontend/public/favicon.svg`, with white ink) and the `TAPP'D` wordmark resolve above the card.
- **1.3–1.9s:** **"Turn Match Data Into Tactical Stories."** rises in below the card, in Barlow Condensed, with "Tactical Stories." in orange.
- **2.1–2.6s:** The call to action pops in: an orange pill, **"Build your first clip →"**.
- **2.6–4.5s:** Hold.
- **4.5–5.0s:** Fade to black, so the video loops cleanly into Act 0's black title card.
